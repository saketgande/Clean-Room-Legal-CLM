## Non-Compete Clauses After Employment in India **Bottom line:** Post-employment non-compete clauses are generally unenforceable in India. Indian courts have consistently held that such restraints fall foul of Section 27 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872, which voids agreements in restraint of trade. --- ### The Governing Rule **Section 27, Indian Contract Act, 1872** provides: > "Every agreement by which any one is restrained from exercising a lawful profession, trade or business of any kind, is to that extent void." The Supreme Court of India affirmed this position in ***Niranjan Shankar Golikari v. Century Spinning & Mfg. Co. Ltd.* (1967)** and subsequent decisions, drawing a sharp distinction between: - **Restraints operative *during* employment** — generally enforceable as part of the service contract; and - **Restraints operative *after* employment ends** — void as an unlawful restraint of trade, regardless of how narrowly they are drafted (geography, duration, or scope). --- ### Application to Common Contract Scenarios | Clause type | During employment | Post-employment | |---|---|---| | Non-compete | Enforceable | **Void** | | Non-solicitation of clients | Enforceable | Likely void (courts split) | | Non-solicitation of employees | Enforceable | Likely void (courts split) | | Confidentiality / trade secrets | Enforceable | **Enforceable** — not a restraint of trade | **Key practical points:** - Courts will not "read down" or blue-pencil an overbroad post-e…

Expected

Explains clearly. No request raised, no tool confirmation.

Got tools: none

A **mutual NDA** (also called a bilateral NDA) imposes confidentiality obligations on **both parties** symmetrically: each side discloses confidential information to the other, and each side is bound to protect what it receives. A **one-way NDA** (unilateral NDA) runs in **one direction only**: only one party discloses, and only the receiving party is bound to keep that information confidential. Here's a quick comparison: | Feature | Mutual NDA | One-Way NDA | |---|---|---| | **Who discloses** | Both parties | One party only | | **Who is bound** | Both parties | Receiving party only | | **Typical use case** | Joint ventures, partnership discussions, M&A due diligence where both sides share sensitive information | Vendor/supplier relationships, employee onboarding, or any situation where only one side shares proprietary information | | **Negotiating leverage** | Symmetric — neither side has an inherent advantage | The disclosing party typically drives the terms | | **Drafting complexity** | Slightly more complex — definitions of "Confidential Information" and obligations must work in both directions | Simpler — obligations flow one way | **Key practical considerations:** - **Scope of "Confidential Information"** matters in both types, but in a mutual NDA you need to ensure the definition works symmetrically and doesn't inadvertently capture one party's information more broadly than the other's. - **Residuals clauses, carve-outs, and permitted disclosures** apply equally in…
